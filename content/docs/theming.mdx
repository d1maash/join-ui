Everything visual in Join UI resolves through CSS custom properties. Change a
token and every component follows — including the ones already installed in your
project.

## The principle

The tokens on this page are the ones that ship: the neutral ramps and the four
semantic hue families declared in `app/globals.css`, which every registry item
carries into your project. Within them there are two layers, and they follow
different rules. The neutral layer — surfaces, type, rules, the filled primary
action — has no brand hue at all.

<Callout variant="note">
  This site renders the dark theme and only the dark theme; there is no
  site-wide switch. Its own chrome — near-black zinc surfaces at `#09090b`,
  hairlines, and a single electric-blue accent, `#2f6bff` — is a separate layer
  in `app/(site)/site.css`, which re-declares the shared neutral tokens on its
  root so the imported blocks match. None of that ships. The tokens below ship
  in both themes, because registry components get installed into other
  people's projects and plenty of those are light. The light values are as real
  and as maintained as the dark ones, and the workbench on a component page can
  flip its preview into a `.light` island to show them.
</Callout>

Leaving the shared tokens without an accent hue is a decision rather than an
omission, and it is worth stating plainly because the obvious alternative is
worse. Pick an accent, and it immediately wants to be everywhere: the link, the
active row, the inline code span, the selected chip, the step number, the focus
ring. Each one is defensible on its own and the result is a page where the
colour has stopped meaning anything, because everything has it. What is left
reads as decoration. That is also why the one blue this site does spend on its
own chrome is held back for the primary action in a view and for marking where
you are — and why a component installed into your project brings no accent
with it. Your brand supplies one, at the emphasis tokens; see
[retuning](#retuning-the-ramp).

So emphasis here is carried by three things that do not have that failure mode:

- **Ink.** The filled dark shape is the loudest thing available, and there is
  usually one of it per view.
- **Material.** A panel sits one step above the page, on a hairline and the
  faintest shadow. That step is what makes a card a card, an active sidebar row
  active, and a selected tab selected — and because it is defined as a
  *direction* rather than a value, it is the same move in both themes.
- **Space.**

The ink never reaches a pure value. Light text is `#1d1d1f` rather than black
and dark text is `#f5f5f7` rather than white, so the interface never draws
`#000` on `#fff` or the reverse, and that is most of what separates this from a
high-contrast editorial system. The surfaces are allowed to: a light card is
pure `#ffffff`, and the dark page is true `#000000`. The strongest pair the
interface can draw is 19.3:1 in dark and 16.8:1 in light (ink on a white card);
body text on the light page is 15.5:1.

Hue is reserved for the places where it *is* the information: the four semantic
families below, used by registry components for a status chip or a progress
marker, by the two serious callout variants, and by syntax highlighting. Even
there it is never allowed to carry the meaning alone.

## The neutral ramp

Twelve steps, from white to ink, and every neutral token below is an alias onto
one of them.

```css title="app/globals.css"
:root,
.light {
  --grey-0: #ffffff; /* cards, popovers */
  --grey-50: #fbfbfd;
  --grey-100: #f5f5f7; /* the page */
  --grey-200: #ececf0;
  --grey-300: #dcdce1; /* the hairline */
  --grey-400: #c7c7cc;
  --grey-500: #aeaeb2;
  --grey-600: #86868b;
  --grey-700: #6e6e73; /* secondary text */
  --grey-800: #424245;
  --grey-900: #2c2c2e;
  --grey-1000: #1d1d1f; /* the ink */
}
```

These are Apple's own greys rather than a derived scale. The page is the
`#f5f5f7` every apple.com band sits on, a card is pure white one step above it,
secondary text is `#6e6e73`, and the ink is `#1d1d1f` rather than black. They
are written as plain hex because they are taken as they are, not computed.

Every step but white carries the same faint cool cast: blue sits two to five units above
red and green, which in `oklch` is a chroma of at most `0.008`, all at one hue
angle. That is far too little to read as a tint and just enough that the greys
are not clinically dead.

The page is not white because the card is. The step from `#f5f5f7` to
`#ffffff` is the system's only elevation cue that works at a glance, and a
white page would have nothing left to lift a card into.

Dark mode does not negate this ramp; it substitutes Apple's dark system greys,
running the same direction. `--grey-0` is the end furthest from the ink in both
themes — white here, true black there — and `--grey-1000` is the ink in both.
Only the surface aliases shift: the light page is `--grey-100` with cards at
`--grey-0`, while the dark page is `--grey-0` with cards at `--grey-50`. The
dark ramp is the one this site renders — see [dark mode](/docs/dark-mode).

## Semantic tokens

Nothing outside this block should reference `--grey-*` directly. Components read
the semantic names, so retuning the ramp retunes the whole interface.

```css title="app/globals.css"
:root,
.light {
  /* Surfaces. A card is white and sits one step above the page. */
  --background: var(--grey-100);
  --foreground: var(--grey-1000);
  --card: var(--grey-0);
  --card-foreground: var(--grey-1000);
  --popover: var(--grey-0);
  --popover-foreground: var(--grey-1000);
  --muted: var(--grey-200);
  --muted-foreground: var(--grey-700);
  --subtle: var(--grey-50);

  /* Rules. Three weights, and each has one job. */
  --border: var(--grey-300); /* the resting hairline */
  --border-hover: var(--grey-400); /* the same hairline, on hover */
  --border-strong: var(--grey-600); /* an emphasis rule; clears 3:1 */
  --input: var(--grey-400);
  --ring: var(--grey-900);

  /* Emphasis is ink. */
  --primary: var(--grey-1000);
  --primary-hover: var(--grey-900);
  --primary-foreground: var(--grey-0);
  --primary-soft: var(--grey-200);
  --accent: var(--grey-1000);
  --accent-foreground: var(--grey-0);
  --accent-soft: var(--grey-200);
  --accent-border: var(--grey-400);
  --secondary: var(--grey-50);
  --secondary-foreground: var(--grey-900);

  /* Chrome status, aliased onto the component hue families. */
  --destructive: var(--critical);
  --destructive-foreground: var(--critical-foreground);
  --destructive-soft: var(--critical-soft);
  --success: var(--positive);
  --success-soft: var(--positive-soft);
  --warning: var(--caution);
  --warning-soft: var(--caution-soft);

  --code-bg: var(--grey-0);
  --selection: rgb(0 113 227 / 0.18);

  --radius: 0.75rem;
}
```

`--selection` is the one neutral-layer token with hue in it: Apple's blue,
`#0071e3`, as an 18% wash in the light theme (the dark theme uses the lighter
`#409cff` at 30%), so a selection looks the way it does everywhere else on the
platform.

`--accent` and `--primary` both resolve to ink and differ only in intent:
`--primary` is a fill, `--accent` is the same ink used as text or as a marker.
They are kept separate because they are the pair you would repoint if you ever
did want a brand hue — see [retuning](#retuning-the-ramp) — and because a
component asking for one should not silently get the other.

`--border-hover` exists for a small reason worth naming. A card's resting border
and its hover border cannot be the same token, or the hover does nothing; with
no accent to switch to, the hover is one step down the ramp plus a slightly
deeper shadow.

Then map them onto utilities with `@theme inline` — see
[Tailwind setup](/docs/tailwind-setup).

## The component palette

Four families carry hue, and each is three tokens with the same shape. `--X` is
the ink: a solid fill, and the colour of text and icons sitting on the tint.
`--X-soft` is that tint, used as a background. `--X-foreground` is whatever sits
on top of the solid `--X` fill.

```css title="app/globals.css"
:root,
.light {
  --info: #0068d1; /* in progress, informational */
  --info-soft: #e8f1fc;
  --info-foreground: #ffffff;

  --positive: #1a7f37; /* success, complete */
  --positive-soft: #e6f4ea;
  --positive-foreground: #ffffff;

  --caution: #b05a00; /* warning */
  --caution-soft: #fdf1e1;
  --caution-foreground: #ffffff;

  --critical: #d70015; /* error, blocked, destructive */
  --critical-soft: #fdeaea;
  --critical-foreground: #ffffff;
}

.dark {
  --info: #409cff;
  --info-soft: #0c2541;
  --info-foreground: #00152e;

  --positive: #30d158;
  --positive-soft: #0d2b16;
  --positive-foreground: #002910;

  --caution: #ffb340;
  --caution-soft: #2f2208;
  --caution-foreground: #2b1700;

  --critical: #ff6961;
  --critical-soft: #35100f;
  --critical-foreground: #330000;
}
```

`--positive` is green and reads as success or complete, `--caution` is amber and
reads as a warning, `--critical` is red and covers error, blocked, and anything
destructive. Each family is an Apple system colour — blue, green, orange, red —
taken at a contrast-safe value rather than its default. In the light theme the
ink is darkened until a white label on the solid fill clears 4.5:1; in the dark
theme it is the lighter, accessible variant of the same colour, and the label on
the fill becomes dark ink. The hues are the familiar ones, so a status reads the
way it does on the platform, and they are kept quiet by area rather than by
chroma: a chip, a marker, a rule, with the `-soft` tint carrying most of the
surface.

The chrome's `--destructive`, `--success` and `--warning` are aliases onto these
families, so a destructive control in the interface and a `--critical` chip
inside a component are the same red.

Every family is declared twice, once in `:root, .light` and once in `.dark`, so
a component never needs a `dark:` variant to pick the right hue. That is not
housekeeping: it is what makes a component portable into a light project, and it
remains a hard rule. See [dark mode](/docs/dark-mode).

All of them are registered in `@theme inline`, so they arrive as ordinary
utilities:

```tsx
<span className="inline-flex items-center gap-1.5 rounded-soft-sm bg-info-soft px-2 py-1 text-info">
  <Loader2 aria-hidden="true" className="size-3.5" />
  In progress
</span>
```

## Token reference

| Token | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `--grey-0` … `--grey-1000` | The ramp. Every neutral token aliases onto it |
| `--background` / `--foreground` | Page surface and body text |
| `--card` / `--card-foreground` | Panel surfaces, one step above the page |
| `--popover` / `--popover-foreground` | Floating surfaces — menus, dialogs, toasts |
| `--muted` / `--muted-foreground` | Recessed fill and secondary text |
| `--subtle` | The quietest fill; asides, table headers |
| `--border` | The resting hairline |
| `--border-hover` | The same hairline one step down, for hover |
| `--border-strong` | Emphasis rule, at or above 3:1 |
| `--input` | Form control boundary |
| `--ring` | Focus outline |
| `--primary` / `--primary-foreground` | The filled ink surface; the primary action |
| `--primary-hover` | Its hover step |
| `--primary-soft` | Flat tint for a selected or held state |
| `--accent` / `--accent-foreground` | The same ink, used as text or as a marker |
| `--accent-soft` / `--accent-border` | Tint and hairline for a held surface |
| `--secondary` / `--secondary-foreground` | Quiet fill with normal ink on top |
| `--destructive` / `--success` / `--warning` | Chrome status; aliases onto the hue families |
| `--info` / `--info-soft` / `--info-foreground` | Component hue: in progress, informational |
| `--positive` / `--positive-soft` / `--positive-foreground` | Component hue: success, complete |
| `--caution` / `--caution-soft` / `--caution-foreground` | Component hue: warning |
| `--critical` / `--critical-soft` / `--critical-foreground` | Component hue: error, blocked, destructive |
| `--code-bg` | Code block fill |
| `--selection` | Text selection wash |
| `--radius` | Base radius for the chrome, `0.75rem` |
| `--radius-soft-sm` / `--radius-soft` / `--radius-soft-lg` | The component radius scale — a tag, a control, a card |
| `--elevation-xs` … `--elevation-lg` | Shadow steps, reached as `shadow-xs` … `shadow-lg` |
| `--duration-*` / `--ease-*` | Motion timing |

## Colour is never the only channel

Hue is available in the component layer, which removes a guard rail a fully
greyscale system would enforce for free. The obligation it enforced still
stands.

<Callout variant="warning">
  A tint is an accelerator, not the message. A chip in `--critical-soft` and one
  in `--positive-soft` differ by hue and very little else, so every state a
  component renders must also carry a glyph **and** a spelled-out label. WCAG
  1.4.1 is satisfied by the icon and the word, never by the fill.
</Callout>

This is also why `StatusBadge` is neutral. Four registry states — `stable`,
`new`, `updated`, `experimental` — were each given a hue at one point, and a
catalog page of them turned into a colour chart in which no colour meant
anything. The words were already doing the work. They are now separated by fill
weight instead, and only `new` is worth interrupting a scan for:

```tsx
<p className="flex items-center gap-2 text-critical">
  <AlertTriangle aria-hidden="true" className="size-4" />
  <span className="font-medium">Payment failed</span>
</p>
```

## Shape and elevation

The chrome has a real radius scale, and the Tailwind steps resolve through it:

```css title="app/globals.css"
@theme inline {
  --radius-xs: 0.375rem;
  --radius-sm: 0.5rem; /* a tag, a chip — rounded-soft-sm */
  --radius-md: 0.625rem;
  --radius-lg: 0.75rem; /* a control; the same as --radius — rounded-soft */
  --radius-xl: 1.125rem; /* a card, a panel — rounded-soft-lg */
  --radius-2xl: 1.375rem;
  --radius-3xl: 1.75rem;
  --radius-4xl: 2.25rem;
}
```

Components round through a scale of their own — `--radius-soft-sm`,
`--radius-soft` and `--radius-soft-lg`, reached as `rounded-soft-sm`,
`rounded-soft` and `rounded-soft-lg`. These are aliases onto `sm`, `lg` and `xl`
above — `0.5rem`, `0.75rem` and `1.125rem` — so anything already installed from
the registry keeps rounding exactly as it did, and a registry item that ships
`cssVars` carries those three values with it. `rounded-full` is untouched in both
layers, because a pip or an avatar is a circle rather than a rounded rectangle.

Elevation is four steps. Each pairs a tight contact shadow with a wide, soft
one, in plain black at low opacity, so a raised surface reads as lifted off the
page rather than outlined. Nothing in the chrome goes above `-md` except a
portal:

```css title="app/globals.css"
:root,
.light {
  --elevation-xs: 0 1px 2px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.04);
  --elevation-sm: 0 1px 2px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.04), 0 4px 14px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.05);
  --elevation-md: 0 2px 6px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.04), 0 12px 32px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.08);
  --elevation-lg: 0 4px 12px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.05), 0 24px 64px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.14);
}

.dark {
  --elevation-xs: 0 1px 2px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.5);
  --elevation-sm: 0 1px 2px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.5), 0 4px 14px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.4);
  --elevation-md: 0 2px 6px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.5), 0 12px 32px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.55);
  --elevation-lg: 0 4px 12px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.5), 0 24px 64px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.7);
}
```

A shadow on a black page cannot darken much, so the dark steps run far deeper
and lean on the hairline a raised surface already carries.

Durations and easings are tokens for the same reason colours are — so timing
stays consistent across components you installed months apart:

```tsx
className="transition-colors duration-[var(--duration-fast)] ease-[var(--ease-out-soft)]"
```

## Retuning the ramp

Retinting the interface is a change to the neutral ramp and nothing else. Edit
the steps, leave the semantic layer alone, and every surface moves:

```css title="app/globals.css"
:root {
  /* Warmer: a paper page and a brown-black ink instead of Apple's cool greys. */
  --grey-100: #f6f3ef; /* the page */
  --grey-300: #e0dbd5; /* the hairline */
  --grey-700: #6c645e; /* secondary text */
  --grey-1000: #211b17; /* the ink */
}
```

Those four are the steps a reader actually sees — page, hairline, secondary
text, ink — and they keep the contrast the system depends on: ink on the page
is 15.4:1 and secondary text 5.2:1. The steps you leave alone stay cool,
so retint the rest of the ramp the same way before you ship it, and restate the
`.dark` ramp if your project has one.

If you do want a brand hue, add it at the emphasis tokens rather than across the
ramp — that is the whole reason `--primary` and `--accent` are separate from
`--foreground`:

```css title="app/globals.css"
:root {
  --primary: #525edd;
  --primary-hover: #414bb5;
  --primary-foreground: #ffffff; /* 5.2:1 on --primary */
  --ring: #6d7bf1;
}
```

<Callout variant="warning">
  Repointing `--accent` as well is the step to think twice about. `--accent` is
  read by links, active navigation, the current TOC entry, selected menu rows
  and list markers, so changing it tints roughly a dozen surfaces at once. That
  is the state this system was deliberately moved away from. Move `--primary`
  first, live with it, and add `--accent` only if the page still needs it.
</Callout>

Keep the neutral ramp's chroma under about `0.02` in `oklch` either way — the
warm example above peaks at `0.014`, Apple's greys at `0.008`. Past that it
stops reading as a warm or cool neutral and starts competing with whatever hue
the components carry.

## Scoped themes

Tokens are inherited custom properties, so any subtree can override them. This
is a supported pattern, not a hack — inverting a section is a token swap:

```tsx
<section
  style={{
    "--background": "var(--grey-1000)",
    "--foreground": "var(--grey-0)",
    "--border": "var(--grey-700)",
  } as React.CSSProperties}
>
  <YourComponent>Inverted panel</YourComponent>
</section>
```

This is the same mechanism that lets a component installed in a light project
theme correctly without a single `dark:` variant — see
[dark mode](/docs/dark-mode).

## Component-level overrides

Every component merges `className` last through `cn`, so `twMerge` resolves the
conflict in your favour:

```tsx
<YourComponent className="rounded-2xl border-border-strong bg-subtle p-8" />
```

Where a component owns more than one surface, it exposes a second class hook
rather than making you fight selectors:

```tsx
<YourComponent className="border-border-strong" innerClassName="bg-subtle p-8" />
```

## Contrast

The headline pairs, measured against the resolved values above:

| Pair | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--foreground` on `--background` | 15.5:1 | 17.0:1 |
| `--foreground` on `--card` | 17.7:1 | 15.4:1 |
| `--muted-foreground` on `--background` | 5.9:1 | 8.8:1 |
| `--muted-foreground` on `--card` | 6.8:1 | 8.0:1 |
| `--muted-foreground` on `--muted` | 5.5:1 | 6.3:1 |
| `--primary-foreground` on `--primary` | 17.7:1 | 17.0:1 |
| `--border-strong` on `--background` | 3.5:1 | 3.8:1 |

The headline pair tops out around 17:1 rather than the 21:1 a black-on-white
system gets, and that is the point — it is the deliberate cost of surfaces that
stop short of both extremes.

`--muted-foreground` is the token to watch. It carries most of the body copy and
it is the one people lighten first when a page feels heavy; in the light theme
it has roughly two steps of headroom above 4.5:1 and no more. The dark theme has
more room, which is a trap rather than a licence — light text on a dark surface
looks heavier than it measures, so the temptation there runs the other way.

The four component families are tuned so their ink clears 4.5:1 against their
own tint, which is the pairing that actually ships:

| Pair | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--info` on `--info-soft` | 5.3:1 | 6.3:1 |
| `--positive` on `--positive-soft` | 5.3:1 | 6.9:1 |
| `--caution` on `--caution-soft` | 4.9:1 | 7.2:1 |
| `--critical` on `--critical-soft` | 5.1:1 | 5.5:1 |

`--caution` has the least margin of the four. If you deepen a `-soft` tint,
deepen its ink in the same commit.

Two ratios in this system are deliberately low and are not failures. `--card`
over `--background` is about 1.1:1 and `--border` on `--card` about 1.4:1 —
neither is text, and both are doing the job a shadow and a hairline are supposed
to do at a glance rather than under measurement. Where a boundary is the only
thing identifying an interactive control, pair it with a fill or reach for
`--border-strong`, which clears 3:1.
